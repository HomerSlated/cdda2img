# Orange Forum and CDs21 Solutions: Background and Leadership

## Overview

**The Orange Forum** (オレンジフォーラム, `orangeforum.or.jp`) was a formally registered Japanese industry organization (`.or.jp` domain denotes non-profit registered entities under Japanese law) established in **March 1996** to promote Orange Book-compliant CD-R/RW products and related technologies.

The organization later merged with the **Multimedia CD Consortium** to form **CDs21 Solutions** in **April 2001**.

### Purpose

From the Orange Forum's own overview page (Japanese):

> 「オレンジフォーラムとは、オレンジブックに準拠した CD-R/RWドライブ、CD-R/RWディスク並びにそれらの関連ハードウエア/ソフトウエア製品の普及促進を図ることを目的に、メーカー 49社で組織されている団体です。」

**Translation:** "The Orange Forum is an organization of 49 manufacturers with the purpose of promoting the spread of CD-R/RW drives, CD-R/RW discs and related hardware/software products that comply with the Orange Book."

## CDs21 Solutions (2001–2012+)

### Formation and Leadership

**CDs21 Solutions** was created in **April 2001** via the merger of:
1. **Orange Forum** (CD-R/RW specialist)
2. **Multimedia CD Consortium** (promoting CD-i and Video CD)

The new organization was designed to coordinate all CD-related standards and promotion activities under a single umbrella.

### Activities

From the CDs21 Solutions mission statement (October 2002):

CDs21 Solutions was a **voluntary association** organized to promote CDs in all their various formats:

- **CD-Audio** (1982 debut; conventional record replacement)
- **Photo CD**
- **CD-ROM**
- **Video CD**
- **CD-i**
- **CD-R/RW** (focus area inherited from Orange Forum)

Primary activities included:

1. **Technical standards development** — coordination across multiple CD standards bodies
2. **Compatibility assurance** — ensuring interoperability between media, drives, and software
3. **New media research** — including "Optical Card" (card-sized discs playable on CD players)
4. **User enlightenment and protection** — standardized user manuals and caution notes
5. **Industry coordination** — working with media makers, drive makers, content providers, and software developers

### Key Initiatives

#### Optical Card Standards (2002)

CDs21 Solutions organized working groups to establish guidelines for "Optical Card" (card-type discs playable on CD players and CD-ROM drives):

- Examined existing instruction manuals
- Prepared standardized "Notes of Caution" (in Japanese and English)
- Distributed materials to member companies
- Aimed at user protection and compatibility assurance

## Relationship to the Orange Book Embargo

### The ATIP Manufacturer Code Registry

CDs21 Solutions (via its Orange Forum predecessor) maintained the authoritative registry of:

- **Media Identification Code (MID)** — 16-bit code comprising a 12-bit manufacturer code + 4-bit variant (stored in the ATIP pregroove of CD-R media)
- **Manufacturer identification** — cross-reference between codes and company names

This registry was controlled and not freely published, leading to the "orange forum embargo" referenced in Jörg Schilling's `cdrtools` source code. Schilling documented the unavailability of current manufacturer code data in comments like:

> "Manufacturer is guessed because of the orange forum embargo. The orange forum likes to get money for recent information."

### The Commercial Successor Model

The transition from Orange Forum (`.or.jp` non-profit) to CDs21 Solutions (`.org` commercial entity) reflected a shift toward commercialized access to technical registry data. CDs21 Solutions maintained password-protected technical sections on its website, a departure from the earlier non-profit model.

## Relevance to CD-DA Ripping

### For `cdda2img` Project

The Orange Forum / CDs21 Solutions organization is relevant for:

1. **ATIP/MID parsing** — if future work adds support for parsing CD-R media identification codes, this organization's registry represents the authoritative (but commercially restricted) source
2. **CD-R Compatibility** — understanding drive/media compatibility information archived by these bodies
3. **Optical media standardization** — the technical work on CD standards that underpin CD-DA

### Manufacturer Code Access

The pragmatic open-source approach (as documented in Schilling's `cdrtools`):

- Ship a best-effort manufacturer table from publicly available sources
- Acknowledge manufacturer code uncertainty in user-visible messages
- Do not represent guessed identifications as authoritative
- Cite source documentation per entry

### AccurateRip and Verification

AccurateRip verification (primary use case in `cdda2img`) is unaffected by the MID registry embargo — the AccurateRip protocol is well-documented externally and does not require ATIP manufacturer code access.

## Notes and Observations

1. **Language barrier:** Most Orange Forum documentation is in Japanese; English coverage is minimal. The organization predates extensive English-language web indexing.

2. **Heitaro NAKAJIMA:** Documented as Chairman of CDs21 Solutions as of October 2002. Associated with Philips operations in Japan. Additional biographical details (career history, education, later roles) not recovered from publicly available English-language sources.

3. **Wataru Fujihira:** General Affairs contact for CDs21 Solutions as of October 2002. Likely involved in membership coordination and administration.

4. **Geographic expansion:** Orange Forum (Japanese organization) evolved into CDs21 Solutions with significant overseas membership (26 of 79 companies), indicating broader industry coordination under a Japanese-led umbrella organization.

5. **Commercialization trajectory:** The shift from Orange Forum (non-profit `.or.jp` entity) to CDs21 Solutions (commercial `.org` entity) reflects industry consolidation and commercialization of technical standards access — the core complaint in Schilling's "embargo" commentary.

6. **Successor status:** No confirmed successor to CDs21 Solutions has been identified. By 2012, the organization was likely winding down as CD-R/RW manufacturing became economically marginal. Any remaining institutional knowledge may reside with former member companies or in archived technical standards documents.
